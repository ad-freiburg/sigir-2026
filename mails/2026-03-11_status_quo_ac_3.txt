Some of you have asked how to handle AI-generated reviews or meta-reviews. We have been running all submissions, reviews, and meta-reviews through an AI checker, namely Pangram. Given a text, Pangram computes three fractions that sum to 1: the fraction it thinks was written by AI, the fraction it thinks was assisted by AI, and the fraction it thinks was written by a human. Pangram is known to have very few false positives, that is, it will almost never flag text written by a human as written by AI. If it errs, it errs on the other side.

When a review or meta-review is flagged as fully written (not just assisted) by AI by Pangram, **there now is an exclamation mark behind the score of that review or behind the recommendation of that meta-review**, respectively. Clicking on that exclamation mark will take you to the respective review or meta-review. Sometimes, OpenReview does not automatically scroll to the review; in that case, reloading the page usually helps.

For the reviews, 16% have been flagged as fully written by AI. For the meta-reviews, currently 37% have been flagged as fully written by AI. Please take this as a hint, not a verdict. In some cases, it is merely the language that was generated by AI, in other cases, the AI did more than that. Please judge for yourself whether the respective SPC should be challenged and asked to produce a more meaningful meta-review. Keep in mind that authors will not be happy to receive meta-reviews that are obviously AI-generated. 

At the end of this mail, we have included once more the instructions from the meta-review form [1]. In particular, we explicitly asked SPCs to **not** just summarize or average the reviews, but to carefully "review the reviews" and separate the wheat from the chaff.

**[1] Instructions at the top of the form for the meta-reviews:**

The meta-review is key for the review process and serves two purposes: it helps the program chairs to arrive at an accept/reject decision, and it explains that accept/reject decision to the authors. Please make sure that your meta-review is informative to both audiences. Specifically:                                                                                                                               
1. Your meta-review should not merely summarize the reviews, but "separate the wheat from the chaff". That is, you should carefully weigh the different arguments, which entails discarding arguments that lack evidence or are unconvincing.
2. If there is substantial disagreement between the reviewers, your meta-review should explain how input from the reviewers factored into the decision. Also factor in your own understanding of the paper, but please be aware of your own biases.
3. Provide actionable feedback for the camera-ready submission or resubmission of the paper. If such feedback is provided in the reviews, you don't have to copy it to your meta-review, but you can refer to it with a short summary.
4. If parts of the explanation are sensitive and should not be provided to the authors, please enter it in the "confidential remarks" section instead.

Again, high-quality meta-reviews are crucial for the whole review process. Thank you!
